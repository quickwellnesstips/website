Why you're tired all the time (and it's not sleep)

If you're sleeping 7+ hours and still dragging, the problem is usually one of these four things. None of them are what the supplement industry is selling you.

By Ray · Updated this week

Most people who come to me for "energy issues" are already sleeping 7+ hours a night and still dragging. The supplement industry is happy to sell them ashwagandha, rhodiola, and B-vitamin complexes. None of it works. The actual reasons you're tired are boring, structural, and fixable in a week.

The four usual suspects

When I audit someone's energy, the answer is almost always one of these:

  1. They're under-eating protein. The most common. Most people eat 0.6-0.8g/kg of protein per day. The energy floor is 1.2g/kg. The 30-50g daily gap shows up as 3pm crashes.
  2. They drink caffeine too late. After 2pm, caffeine's half-life (5-6 hours) means you're still processing it at midnight. Your sleep gets 90 minutes shorter even if you don't feel it.
  3. They don't get morning sunlight. Bright outdoor light in the first 30-60 minutes after waking sets your circadian rhythm. Without it, your melatonin ramps 2-3 hours late and your sleep quality drops 15-20%.
  4. They over-caffeinate to compensate for the first three. This is the trap. Each of the first three problems is "solved" with coffee, which then makes all three worse.

The fix (one week)

You don't need supplements. You need to remove the caffeine, hit the morning light, and add 30g of protein at breakfast. Here's the day-by-day:

Day 1-2: Move your last coffee to noon. Notice how tired you get at 3pm. (This is the un-masking. The crash was always there — coffee was just hiding it.)

Day 3-4: Add 10 minutes of morning sunlight. Phone outside, no sunglasses, before 9am if possible. (Bonus: 5 minutes of walking in it.)

Day 5-6: Add 30g protein to breakfast. Eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or a scoop of protein powder. Whatever you'll actually eat.

Day 7: Compare to last Monday. If you're not noticeably better, something else is going on — see a doctor and ask for a blood panel (ferritin, vitamin D, thyroid, B12).

The bottom line

Energy isn't a supplement problem. It's a systems problem. Fix the inputs (sleep, light, food, caffeine timing) and the output (energy) follows. The supplement industry makes money when you believe the opposite.
